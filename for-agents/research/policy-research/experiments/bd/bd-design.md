# Beautiful Design Policy

Design exists to make use easier.

Good design is not decoration added after meaning. It is the quiet arrangement
of meaning so the reader, user, or maintainer can proceed without friction.

This policy follows a simple test. If a design choice does not make the thing
more useful, more understandable, more honest, or more durable, remove it.

## Principles

Prefer usefulness over expression.

Prefer clarity over novelty.

Prefer restraint over display.

Prefer ordinary structure over clever presentation.

Prefer details that disappear into correct behavior.

Prefer small visual systems that survive many renderers.

## Rules

A design must not promise more than the thing can do.

A design must not require explanation before use.

A design must not depend on color, motion, size, or layout alone to carry
meaning.

A design must remain usable when the ornamental layer is removed.

A design must give the user room to read, act, and think.

## Checks

Read it on a small screen.

Read it without styling.

Read it with links, headings, and lists exposed.

Remove one visual choice. Keep removing until removal hurts.

When removal hurts, keep the last useful thing.

## Source Spirit

The reference is Dieter Rams by way of Vitsoe: useful, understandable,
unobtrusive, honest, long-lasting, careful, resource-aware, and reduced to the
essential.
